Oil-Spill Detection & Lookalike Discrimination

Capillary wave damping physics, adaptive thresholding, and lookalike rejection

1. Radar Capillary Wave Damping Physics

Microwave radar backscatter from sea surfaces is primarily governed by Bragg scattering with short centimeter-scale surface capillary waves. Hydrocarbon films dampen these high-frequency capillary waves, dramatically reducing backscatter return.

2. The Lookalike Discrimination Challenge

Natural biogenic slicks (algal blooms, fish oils), low-wind calm patches, grease ice, and internal waves also appear as dark formations in SAR imagery. TRACEOIL filters out lookalikes using:

  • Dual-Polarization Ratios: Mineral oil alters the cross-polarization ratio distinctly from natural monomolecular films.
  • ECMWF Wind Screening: Rejecting calm areas where ambient 10m wind speed is below 2.5 m/s.
  • Morphological Plume Metrics: Assessing elongation and tail tapering typical of moving vessel discharge plumes.
