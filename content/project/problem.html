1. Foundations

Problem Understanding & Theoretical Challenges

The physics of monocular depth estimation, nadir perspective domain gaps, and scale-offset ambiguity.

1. Limitations of Conventional Elevation Capture

High-resolution Digital Elevation Models (DEMs) and Digital Surface Models (DSMs) are fundamental to modern environmental governance, civil engineering, disaster flood modeling, and tactical reconnaissance. However, existing capture pipelines present severe operational bottlenecks:

  • Airborne LiDAR: Extremely high precision (±0.05m) but cost-prohibitive, requiring dedicated flight corridors, good weather, and heavy sensor logistics.
  • Stereo-Optical Photogrammetry: Demands multiple overlapping satellite passes from identical orbital trajectories, introducing significant latency and cloud cover vulnerability.
  • InSAR (Interferometric Radar): Sensitive to surface decoherence, atmospheric delay, and steep terrain layover distortions.

2. The Nadir Remote-Sensing Domain Gap

Foundational computer vision models are predominantly trained on horizontal perspective datasets (indoor scenes, autonomous driving). When applied to top-down (nadir) satellite imagery, conventional perspective cues (vanishing points, horizon compression) are completely absent. Depth estimation must rely entirely on solar cast shadows, roof edge geometry, and texture frequency.

3. The Scale-Offset Ambiguity

Monocular depth estimation is mathematically ill-posed: an infinite set of real-world 3D geometries can project onto the exact same 2D pixel array. Neural models predict relative depth normalized to arbitrary internal tensors. Converting these features into true metric heights in meters requires an external geospatial anchor (SRTM 30m) coupled with semantic material compensation.
