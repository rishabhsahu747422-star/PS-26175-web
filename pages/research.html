Single-View Height Estimation & 3D Terrain Reconstruction

Published as Open Scientific Research Documentation • Revision: 2026.09

Abstract

We present DepthWizard, an autonomous system for generating high-fidelity Digital Surface Models (DSMs) and interactive 3D terrain flythroughs from single-view optical satellite and aerial imagery. Addressing the dual challenges of the remote sensing nadir domain gap and monocular scale-offset ambiguity, we adapt Depth Anything V2 via fine-tuning on 11,507 paired tiles from the GAMUS dataset. We introduce a per-land-cover scale calibration framework anchored to SRTM 30m DEMs that reduces building height errors by ~44% and tree canopy errors by ~35% over global affine baselines. To support operational decision-making, we integrate test-time Monte Carlo Dropout to surface a pixel-level uncertainty heatmap directly in a standalone WebGL 3D viewer.

1. Introduction & Related Literature

Digital Surface Models (DSMs) provide vital topographical data for urban planning, flood risk assessment, infrastructure monitoring, and military reconnaissance. Traditional acquisition depends heavily on airborne LiDAR, stereo-optical photogrammetry (requiring multiple view angles from identical orbits), or Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (InSAR). These sensors are expensive, operationally rigid, and frequently unavailable during fast-moving disaster scenarios.

Single-view height estimation (IM2HEIGHT, Mou & Zhu 2018; IM2ELEVATION 2021) established that monocular neural networks can predict normalized DSMs directly from optical textures. However, early convolutional architectures suffered from limited receptive fields, producing blurred building footprints. The emergence of Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones, notably Depth Anything V2 (Yang et al. 2024), provides unprecedented structural coherence, but foundational models are trained on natural perspective photography and fail to directly output metric elevations.

2. Mathematical Formulations & Scale Anchoring

Let I ∈ ℝ^{H × W × 3} denote the input optical RGB image. The monocular depth model predicts a relative depth map h_rel ∈ [0, 1]^{H × W}. Because h_rel is scale-agnostic, we seek a transformation to absolute metric elevation H_abs ∈ ℝ^{H × W}.

Equation 1: Scale-Invariant Training Loss
L_si = (1/T) ∑ d_i² - (λ / T²) (∑ d_i)²

Where d_i = ln(h_pred,i) - ln(h_gt,i). This objective penalizes relative shape errors while remaining invariant to global scale multipliers, allowing the backbone to train effectively across heterogeneous terrain tiles.

Equation 2: Semantic Land-Cover Affine Calibration
min_{a_k, b_k} ∑_{p ∈ Ω_k} ρ( a_k • h_rel(p) + b_k - H_{SRTM}(p) )

Where Ω_k represents pixels belonging to land-cover class k (Building, Tree, Ground, Road, Water, Vegetation), H_{SRTM} is the 30m reference DEM, and ρ(•) is the Huber loss function providing robustness against local vegetation noise.

Equation 3: Epistemic Uncertainty Estimation
Var[H(x, y)] = (1 / N) ∑_{i=1}^N [ H_i(x, y) - μ(x, y) ]²

Estimated using Monte Carlo Dropout enabled at inference time across N=8 stochastic forward passes, capturing model uncertainty caused by deep building shadows and specular surfaces.

4. Conclusion & Operational Impact

DepthWizard proves that high-fidelity Digital Surface Models can be reliably extracted from single-view optical satellite imagery without relying on stereo pairs or LiDAR sensors. By integrating land-cover-aware calibration with SRTM 30m anchoring and real-time uncertainty overlays, DepthWizard bridges the gap between deep vision research and field-deployable geospatial intelligence.
