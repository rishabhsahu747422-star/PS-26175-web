TRACEOIL: Oil Spill Detection & Vessel Attribution

Autonomous multi-modal satellite remote sensing, hydrodynamic reverse drift modeling, and AIS vessel trajectory matching.

Research Overview: TRACEOIL is an open scientific research initiative for Smart India Hackathon 2026. The platform investigates automated methodologies for detecting marine oil spill discharges and establishing provable, time-correlated attribution to responsible maritime vessels using Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR), multi-spectral optical data, and historical AIS transponder telemetry.

Research Motivation & Context

Why Maritime Oil Spill Attribution Matters

Marine oil pollution inflicts catastrophic, long-lasting damage on oceanic ecosystems, coastal coral reefs, and fisheries. Illegal bilge dumping and accidental discharges frequently go unpunished due to jurisdictional ambiguities and attribution lag.

Deliberate Bilge Discharges

Over 80% of marine oil contamination results from operational tank-cleaning and illicit oily wastewater dumping under the cover of darkness, evading traditional maritime patrols.

Operational Challenge

Attribution Lag & Drift

By the time a slick is discovered, oceanic currents and wind forcing have displaced the oil miles from the release point, making direct visual linking to passing ships impossible without hydrodynamic drift modeling.

Physical Challenge

Enforceable Forensics

Regulatory agencies and maritime coast guards require mathematically rigorous, time-synchronized evidentiary packages capable of holding rogue vessel operators accountable under international maritime law (MARPOL 73/78).

Regulatory Impact
The TRACEOIL Approach

Rather than treating oil spill detection and vessel surveillance as isolated tasks, TRACEOIL couples spaceborne radar imaging, automated ship extraction, reverse-time ocean current advection, and AIS corridor reconstruction into a unified, reproducible forensic chain.

Core Objectives

Research Goals & Deliverables

The primary aim of TRACEOIL under the Smart India Hackathon 2026 framework is to develop a scientific and technical baseline for sovereign coastal surveillance and environmental law enforcement.

  • 01.
    All-Weather Slick Detection: Leverage day-and-night C-band Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) to identify capillary wave suppression without dependence on daylight or clear skies.
  • 02.
    Lookalike Damping Discrimination: Isolate true hydrocarbon spills from biogenic monomolecular slicks, low-wind grease areas, and internal waves using dual-polarization ratios and wind-speed thresholds.
  • 03.
    Reverse-Time Drift Reconstruction: Backtrack slick movement through reverse Lagrangian particle physics fueled by ocean surface currents (Copernicus CMEMS) and ECMWF surface wind fields.
  • 04.
    Automated AIS Spatiotemporal Corroboration: Intersect backtracked slick origin corridors with historical vessel tracks to rank candidate vessels with confidence scores.
Key Research Questions

Scientific Hypotheses

Q1: Lookalike Discrimination

Can cross-polarization ratio \(\sigma^0_{VH} / \sigma^0_{VV}\) combined with ECMWF wind speed bounds reliably segregate biogenic film slicks from crude oil discharges?

Q2: Drift Error Quantification

What is the maximum backward time horizon (e.g., 6h, 12h, 24h) within which reverse Lagrangian advection preserves a vessel attribution confidence exceeding 90%?

Q3: Dark Ship Countermeasures

When a delinquent vessel intentionally disables its AIS transponder, can SAR CFAR geometric length and wake angle analysis reconstruct the missing vessel track?
