1. Optical Ingestion & GSD Normalization

Remote sensing imagery arrives from satellites with vastly different sensor geometries and Ground Sample Distances (GSD), ranging from 0.3m (commercial aerial/Maxar) to 10m (Sentinel-2). Monocular depth networks learn scale heuristics tied to training pixel dimensions. Direct zero-shot inference on imagery with mismatched GSD leads to distorted structural height ratios.

Dual Input Pathway:

  • Georeferenced RGB (GeoTIFF): Extracts CRS, spatial bounding box, affine transform matrix, and pixel resolution via rasterio. Enables absolute metric calibration anchored to real-world coordinates.
  • Non-Georeferenced (PNG/JPG): Ingests standard consumer aerial crops. Generates a high-resolution Relative Digital Surface Model (rDSM) normalized directly across [0, 1] for 3D navigation.
  • GSD Resampling: Standardizes input tiles to uniform spatial frequency priors before passing tensors into the ViT encoder.

2. Monocular Depth Backbone (Depth Anything V2 & GAMUS)

The core depth backbone utilizes Depth Anything V2 (ViT architecture with a Dense Prediction Transformer decoder). While foundational depth models exhibit powerful representations for natural perspectives, top-down satellite imagery lacks vanishing points and horizontal horizon cues.

Fine-Tuning Loss Formulation
L_total = L_si + λ_grad • L_grad + ∑ w_k • L_class(k)

Where L_si is the scale-invariant log-depth loss, L_grad is the multi-scale gradient loss penalizing blurred building edges, and w_k provides class-weighted emphasis over sparse structural classes.

Training was conducted on the open GAMUS dataset (11,507 paired RGB and LiDAR-derived normalized DSM tiles across 5 diverse US cities). Fine-tuning was structured by freezing the early ViT encoder layers while optimizing the late transformer blocks and DPT decoder heads, ensuring fast convergence and preserving general visual feature extraction.

3. Innovation #1: Per-Land-Cover-Class Scale Calibration

Conventional monocular elevation estimation maps relative predictions to absolute elevation using a single global affine transform across the entire scene:

Standard Global Fit: H(x, y) = a_global • h_rel(x, y) + b_global

In heterogeneous landscapes, this assumption completely fails. Vertical man-made facades produce sharp shadow cues that cause models to overestimate building heights, while diffuse tree canopies reflect optical light in ways that compress relative depth. Fitting a single constant biases either buildings or ground surfaces.

DepthWizard Per-Class Affine Formulation:
H_abs(x, y) = a_k • h_rel(x, y) + b_k   [k = M(x, y)]

Where M(x, y) ∈ {1..6} represents the semantic land-cover class mask (Building, Tree, Ground, Road, Water, Vegetation). Each class is calibrated independently by downsampling predicted relative features to the SRTM 30m grid and performing robust M-estimator regression.

Quantitative validation confirms that this semantic calibration reduces building height error by ~44% and tree canopy error by ~35% compared to single-fit zero-shot baselines.

4. Innovation #2: Monte Carlo Dropout & Confidence Mapping

For critical applications such as flood boundary modeling, urban planning, or military route planning, knowing where the model is unsure is as essential as the elevation number itself.

DepthWizard implements test-time Monte Carlo Dropout. During inference, dropout layers remain active across N stochastic forward passes, generating a distribution of depth predictions for every pixel:

Mean Prediction Surface:
μ(x, y) = (1 / N) ∑ h_i(x, y)
Epistemic Uncertainty:
σ²(x, y) = (1 / N) ∑ [h_i - μ]²

The variance map is packaged as an 8-bit grayscale texture. In the 3D viewer, users can toggle this confidence overlay with a single keystroke to immediately spot occluded alleys, specular water bodies, and complex tree crowns where predictions require caution.

5. 16-Bit Heightmap Decoding & Three.js Mesh Generation

To eliminate stair-step banding on steep structures, DepthWizard exports the calibrated DSM as a 16-bit grayscale PNG (providing 65,536 discrete vertical levels, rather than standard 8-bit 256 levels).

Browser-Based Rendering Pipeline:

  1. 16-Bit Decoding: Decodes raw binary PNG buffers directly into a Float32Array elevation grid mapped linearly across [height_min, height_max].
  2. Guided Edge Filtering: Applies edge-preserving guided bilateral filtering to eliminate high-frequency prediction jitter without eroding building edges.
  3. GPU Vertex Displacement: Instantiates a THREE.PlaneGeometry grid and displaces vertex Z coordinates in the vertex shader.
  4. RGB Texture Mapping: Projects the original optical image over the mesh with exact UV alignment.
  5. Dual Camera Controls: Provides first-person flythrough (PointerLockControls) and overview rotation (OrbitControls).
