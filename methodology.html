1. Data Acquisition & Multi-Modal Feeds

TRACEOIL interfaces with public earth observation catalogues and maritime telemetry streams. Primary feeds include:

  • Copernicus Sentinel-1 SAR: Level-1 Ground Range Detected (GRD) in Interferometric Wide (IW) swath mode, featuring dual polarization (VV + VH) with a 10-meter pixel spacing and 250 km swath width.
  • Copernicus Sentinel-2 Optical: Level-2A Bottom-Of-Atmosphere (BOA) reflectance across 13 spectral bands for daytime optical confirmation.
  • Terrestrial & Satellite AIS: Standard ITU-R M.1371 NMEA 0183 decoded messages supplying timestamped latitude, longitude, speed over ground (SOG), course over ground (COG), ship type, MMSI, and dimensions.
  • MetOcean Hydrodynamics: Copernicus Marine (CMEMS) Global Ocean Analysis and Forecast (hourly surface current velocities \(u, v\)) and ECMWF ERA5/IFS 10-meter surface wind vectors.

2. Satellite Imagery Preprocessing

Raw SAR intensity files cannot be used directly for computer vision segmentation due to speckle noise and radiometric distortions. The preprocessing pipeline applies five sequential transformations:

Orbit File Correction

Applies precise restituted orbit state vectors from ESA to fix geometric satellite positioning down to centimeter precision.

Thermal Noise Removal

Normalizes background thermal noise across sub-swath boundaries in both co- and cross-polarization channels.

Radiometric Calibration

Converts raw digital pixel numbers into physical radar backscatter coefficient \(\sigma^0\) (Sigma Naught) in decibels (dB).

Speckle Filtering

Applies a Refined Lee filter (7x7 window) to suppress granular multiplicative speckle while preserving sharp linear slick boundaries.

Finally, a land-masking pass removes terrestrial and shallow estuarine reflections using high-resolution Global Self-consistent, Hierarchical, High-resolution Geography (GSHHG) coastline shapefiles buffered by 500 meters.

3. Dark Formation & Oil Spill Detection

Because oil slicks dampen capillary waves, their backscatter intensity is significantly lower than that of the surrounding undisturbed sea. The detection stage extracts candidate dark formations:

Click to preview
Figure 2: Adaptive intensity thresholding segmenting oil spill core from the background clutter.

An adaptive local threshold \(T\) is evaluated across moving regional sub-windows:

\(T = \mu_{\text{local}} - k \cdot \sigma_{\text{local}}\)

Where \(\mu_{\text{local}}\) and \(\sigma_{\text{local}}\) are the mean and standard deviation of background sea clutter, and \(k\) is a sensitivity parameter calibrated to local wind speeds.

4. Morphological & Polarimetric Feature Extraction

Once candidate dark formations are segmented, a feature descriptor vector \(\vec{F}\) is computed for each polygon to discriminate true oil spills from natural lookalikes:

Feature Category Extracted Metric Relevance in Discrimination
Geometric Area, Perimeter, Compactness \((P^2 / 4\pi A)\) Separates elongated plume discharges from round biogenic patches
Morphological Elongation, Skeleton Major Axis, Curvature Reflects trailing wake patterns typical of moving ship discharges
Physical / Radiometric Damping Ratio: \(\Delta \sigma^0 = \sigma^0_{\text{sea}} - \sigma^0_{\text{slick}}\) True oil exhibits >7 dB damping; biogenic film typically 3–6 dB
Polarimetric VV/VH Dual-Polarization Ratio Cross-polar channel discriminates rough water volume scattering
Environmental ECMWF 10m Wind Velocity Magnitude Filters out zero-wind zones (<2.5 m/s) where lookalikes dominate

5. Vessel Detection & AIS Identification

Metallic ships act as strong trihedral corner reflectors in SAR imagery, generating intensely bright backscatter spikes that contrast sharply with the sea surface.

The target Cell Under Test (CUT) is declared a ship if its backscatter exceeds the threshold computed from adjacent training cells (while skipping immediate guard cells to prevent target energy leakage).

6. Reverse Drift Modeling & Attribution Scoring

Because oil drifts downwind and with ocean currents, its observed location at time \(T_{\text{sat}}\) does not correspond to its release point. TRACEOIL executes a reverse Lagrangian particle tracking simulation:

Click to preview
Figure 4: Copernicus CMEMS ocean surface currents \((u, v)\) coupled with ECMWF 10m wind fields forcing reverse slick trajectory.

The slick polygon is discretized into an ensemble of 500 virtual particles. Each particle is advected backward in time through numerical integration of hourly Copernicus current vectors and 3.2% wind leeway. The resulting backward trajectory forms a probability corridor of genesis.

Every vessel whose AIS track crosses this spatiotemporal corridor within a time tolerance \(\Delta t \le 30\) minutes is scored using the attribution probability metric:

\(P_{\text{attribution}}(V_i) = \exp\left( - \frac{d(\vec{x}_{\text{back}}(t), \vec{x}_{\text{vessel}}(t))^2}{2\sigma_s^2} \right) \cdot \exp\left( - \frac{\Delta t^2}{2\sigma_t^2} \right) \cdot K_{\text{anomaly}}\)
